Organize a Severance Offer Before Seeking Qualified Advice

Prepare a dated record of the offer, attachments, employer communications and questions for a qualified severance review.

Reviewed November 23, 2026. This article provides general legal information. Consult qualified legal counsel for advice about your circumstances.

When you receive an offer, record the exact version and all documents it references. Write the date and method of receipt and identify any time request, without deciding what legal effect it has. Seek qualified counsel promptly for advice about the offer, options and relevant timing. The Lacy Employment Law Firm describes severance among its employee-side services. Ask any provider about its current intake and what records it needs. This framework helps you prepare the conversation and preserve privacy. It leaves valuation, legal obligations, interpretation and any decision about agreeing to terms to appropriate qualified review.

Identify what was actually provided

List the offer, attachments and earlier records it mentions. Put the exact date and source beside each item. If a document refers to a policy, agreement or other material you do not have, identify that gap and ask qualified counsel how to request it appropriately. Preserve the version you received as appropriate. Keep any employer explanation under the event in which it occurred. A short index helps an adviser find the relevant material quickly. Use an appropriate method to protect documents and avoid collecting or sharing sensitive records outside your permission simply because a referenced item may be relevant.

Record dates and requested actions with their sources

Put the date the employer sent the offer, when you received it and any written request for response into separate fields. Keep exact wording available for qualified counsel. If a later communication changes a date, retain the prior version and ask which question needs qualified review. Seek advice promptly. Avoid relying on a casual estimate of time or assuming an offer date applies to another legal issue. Make sure the next action is based on information verified for your situation. Keep the date, source and responsible person connected to each instruction so you can check its status later.

Describe the work history and circumstances briefly

Include the employer entity, work location or locations, position and key dates you can confirm. Identify employment records that support those facts. If earlier agreements or changes are referenced, note the source and version. Keep your own interpretation separate from the documented summary. Ask counsel if another employment or pay record is needed and how to provide it. A factual brief can help the adviser identify documents and client questions without requiring an exhaustive account before it can guide you. Mention any pressing practical decision in the first contact rather than burying it in the complete history.

Turn unclear language into questions for counsel

Mark topics that create an issue for you and identify where they are written. Ask an appropriate lawyer what the terms would mean in your actual circumstances and which information is necessary to advise you. Avoid treating plain-language summaries or another person’s agreement as a reading of your offer. Some questions may involve other records or qualified professionals, which counsel can help identify. Keep your questions in plain words and group them by subject and urgency. Ask how the adviser will communicate recommendations and what further action may be needed if another offer is received.

Decide how employer communications will be tracked

Record who is handling the employer’s offer and how it expects routine communications. Seek qualified advice about responses or actions relating to the terms or timing. Preserve actual requests and answers, including a date and source for any revised document. Keep employer responses distinct from advice you receive. If you agree that another person will handle communications, confirm the scope of their role. Share only relevant information through a verified method. A record prepared at this stage can help you later identify whether a discussion referred to the original offer or to another version with changed terms.

Confirm the next step after the conversation

Ask the qualified adviser to identify what is unresolved and what needs action. Note which information or time considerations you have verified and which questions remain. If another adviser or specialist is involved, identify what help is being sought. Keep communications, records and advice in an appropriate private place. A result such as a further appointment or another verified version of the offer can have its own next action. Record the contact and date so someone advising you later can tell what has actually happened. Update the file as documents or facts change and verify whether earlier guidance still applies.
