Prepare an Employment Records Checklist When Work Ends

Organize notices, final work records, administrative requests and open legal questions during an employment transition.

Reviewed November 23, 2026. This article provides general legal information. Consult qualified legal counsel for advice about your circumstances.

When work ends, identify the records and decisions you may need later. Keep relevant notices, messages and authorized documents in an appropriate private place. Ask the responsible contact how to obtain documents in its ordinary process. Consult qualified counsel before retaining restricted material or making legal decisions about a notice, agreement or timing. Use this checklist to organize administrative tasks and questions. Record the date and source of each confirmed item and mark what is still unknown. A practical exit record helps you describe the circumstances to appropriate people without sharing unnecessary personal, employer or third-party information.

Identify who owns the administrative questions

Find the verified person or office that can answer routine document, pay or other administrative questions. Confirm which channels it wants you to use. Keep a separate list of matters for qualified legal advice. An employer contact may be able to locate a record while an adviser needs to examine how it applies. Give each question a clear description and next action. Save how each person identified its role. If a new contact is assigned, confirm the change and record its date. This structure helps avoid sending a mixed set of records to someone who only needs one part of the information.

Inventory relevant notices and versions

Write the document name, date received and where it can be found. Include any applicable attachment in the index. If the employer supplied subsequent versions, preserve them as appropriate and note when each arrived. A date mentioned in a notice should stay with the source and exact description. Seek advice promptly about timing or an agreement before relying on a general explanation. Use the appropriate procedure to request a missing copy. Keep the legal and factual questions about a document distinguishable from the act of obtaining it. A record can help a qualified adviser see which files exist and which remain uncertain.

Identify work and pay documents by period

List records relating to relevant work and pay periods using items you are permitted to retain. Describe the dates covered by each entry. If you have a question about an amount or adjustment, identify its statement line and ask the appropriate person how the current documentation can be verified. Do not assume a final statement resolves another question. Use qualified advice for legal concerns and keep any personal worksheets distinct from source records. If another organization or work location was involved, label it so the reviewer can consider whether that affects the advice you request.

Record property or account handover as its own activity

Follow the applicable directions for returning property and handling access under the employer's authorized process. Ask who can confirm receipt or completion of the relevant arrangements. Keep an appropriate reference for the dates and facts that concern you. Do not access restricted systems or collect confidential or third-party records to prepare for an enquiry. If information remains relevant but unavailable, note where it may be controlled and seek qualified advice about obtaining it appropriately. A clean record of handover and follow-up can be useful even when a legal or administrative question about the employment remains open.

Keep different future actions from being confused

List any appointment, document request, response or advice enquiry with its own date and recipient. If a deadline or other time-sensitive concern arises, seek qualified guidance promptly. Ask what written confirmation would show the task is done. A message acknowledging receipt may leave a separate decision open. Record the actual status from the relevant contact. Keep records concerning agency processes distinct from internal employer communications. Protect personal or employer information when sharing it through appropriate channels. If you need assistance, prepare a concise summary of the issue and the available evidence for the qualified person reviewing it.

Refresh the record when something changes

Add a new entry for a response, document or change in a planned action. Keep earlier versions available where appropriate and identify which information is current. Ask who has agreed to handle each open issue and what it needs next. Store records securely and keep access limited to appropriate people. Check that each significant date has a source and that questions involving advice go to qualified counsel. A current checklist lets you explain what happened, which tasks were completed and what information is still missing. Make a brief dated note after each meaningful update rather than rewriting your whole employment history.
