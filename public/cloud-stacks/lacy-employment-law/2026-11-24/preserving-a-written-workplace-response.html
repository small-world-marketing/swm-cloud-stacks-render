Keep an Employer Response Attached to the Request It Answers

Track an employer response, the original request and any follow-up question as separate dated records before seeking appropriate advice.

Reviewed November 23, 2026. This article provides general legal information. Consult qualified legal counsel for advice about your circumstances.

Save a written employer response by linking it to the exact request or report it addresses. Note when it was sent, when you received it and whether another communication changed the situation. A response can acknowledge receipt, request information or state a decision. Keep those actions easy to identify without assuming what the response means legally. If an employment action or offer has a time condition, seek qualified advice promptly. This guide helps you preserve the words, attachments and chronology that a reviewer may need, with sensitive records handled through appropriate and permitted channels.

Find the request in its original form

Identify the message, form or other communication that led to the response. Name the process used and who received it. Keep an appropriately held copy or a reference to where it is controlled. If the request was made in conversation, note what you remember, its date and circumstances. Avoid adding facts that emerged later to the original description. Give subsequent explanations their own entries. This allows the reviewer to see the original question and whether the reply addresses the same topic. If the response seems to concern a different event, keep that issue visible rather than changing the file label to fit your assumption.

Record receipt, sender and any stated next action

Save the communication with its appropriate date and description. If it refers to an attachment, identify the exact item that accompanied it and note whether you received it. Put any requested next action in its own entry with the date and person who gave it. Ask qualified counsel about legal timing or consequences where important. A record may distinguish the date of an employer’s communication from the date an employment change takes effect. Keep those dates tied to evidence. Avoid using information from a later message to fill a gap about when the original decision was received.

Preserve the difference between an acknowledgment and a decision

Read the response in terms of what it says happened. If it confirms receipt but gives no outcome, record the outcome as pending. If it states a decision, identify what it applies to and what further review remains open. Ask the relevant contact to clarify a factual point as appropriate and ask qualified counsel about legal implications. Keep the exact response available for people authorized to review it. An internal record may provide a reference number or a process for follow-up. Save that near the response instead of treating the number as proof of a decision it does not describe.

Attach subsequent changes with their own dates

If a later communication changes a detail, create a new entry and describe how it relates to the earlier record. Keep each source available where you are permitted to retain it. Do not merge several explanations into a single quote. If an important date or name differs, write a question for the appropriate person instead of selecting a version without evidence. Record changes to work, meetings or other actions separately as appropriate. A chronology will let a qualified reviewer see when different things were communicated and which part of the situation was known at each stage.

Decide which records require a secure discussion

Review whether the response contains confidential, personal or private information. Before sharing it, ask the authorized recipient what it needs and which method it accepts. If you are unsure whether you may retain material, consult qualified counsel. Keep a description or record reference where that is appropriate. Do not distribute entire messages or attachments unnecessarily. Offer the relevant excerpt or document through the method the reviewer specifies. Record what was shared and with whom in a suitable place. A concise index can help the reviewer identify which versions it needs without circulating more information than necessary.

Use the record to ask a specific next question

Take the original request, response and any update and write one sentence about the point you want clarified. For a legal or time-sensitive issue, contact qualified counsel or the relevant agency promptly and use current information. For an ordinary status enquiry, use the employer's established contact as appropriate. Keep the replies with the subject they address. Give any pending action an owner and identify what can confirm completion. A well organized file means another reviewer can tell what the response actually says, what happened afterward and what part of your question remains unanswered.
