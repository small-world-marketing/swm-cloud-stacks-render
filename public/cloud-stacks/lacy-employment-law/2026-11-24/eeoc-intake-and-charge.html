Keep EEOC Intake and Filing Records Distinct

Use current EEOC guidance to track enquiries, intake appointments and any formal charge confirmation as separate steps.

Reviewed November 23, 2026. This article provides general legal information. Consult qualified legal counsel for advice about your circumstances.

Use the EEOC’s current filing information to ask which process you are beginning and what counts as completion. A call, portal action, appointment or submission can have a specific status. Keep a copy or appropriate reference for each acknowledgement, and ask the agency whether anything further is needed. A qualified lawyer can help evaluate which process and timing constraints apply to your circumstances. This comparison helps you prepare the facts and record responses. Avoid assuming that a step in an employer’s internal process or another agency’s system satisfies an EEOC requirement for your situation.

Begin by stating which step you are trying to verify

Write whether you are seeking process information, arranging an intake conversation or checking the status of an earlier action. Identify the dates actually available to you and the official place where a record appears. Ask the agency to explain the status of the item and any further action it needs. Keep your confirmation separate from suggested next steps. If you need legal advice about coverage, interaction with another process or timing, bring the status to qualified counsel. Current official guidance and an agency response can help identify which question deserves attention before you rely on a particular submission.

Record what each communication acknowledges

Save the date, channel, identifier and the wording the agency used to describe the action. If an appointment was scheduled, keep the confirmation by itself. If you received a document about a submission, ask what status it confirms. Do not edit official wording to fit the type of action you expected. Make a separate note for any question you need answered. If more than one account or case reference exists, verify through an official channel which record concerns your enquiry. Keeping the dates distinct helps a qualified adviser see what was done and which part is still uncertain.

Prepare the work facts that the intake discussion may need

Organize the organization’s name, the relevant work locations and a short chronology of the events you want to discuss. Record where dates and communications came from. Ask the appropriate recipient what documents it needs and how those can be shared securely. Keep privately held notes and instructions separate from what you send. If a document is subject to legal or confidentiality concerns, ask qualified counsel about it before sharing. A small factual brief can give the reviewer enough information to ask focused questions without transferring unnecessary workplace or personal records before its process calls for them.

Ask what timing requirements apply to the particular action

Compare the source dates and explain any uncertainty, such as a difference between when an event occurred and when you received a notice. Consult the agency’s current time-limit information and seek qualified advice about which requirements apply to your circumstances. Avoid assuming permission to wait based on an acknowledgement or a separate process. Ask counsel whether a new event changes the analysis. Get advice promptly if important dates are unclear. Put a verified source, date and action beside any reminder you keep. Make sure your record states which agency or legal process the timing information concerns.

Keep state and internal communications in separate entries

If you have contacted the PHRC or New Jersey’s DCR, record that under that agency's own reference. Do the same for an employer report. Ask the relevant agencies or qualified counsel about any relationship between the processes and what must be verified. Avoid assuming that a record in one system establishes the status in another. Note who answered your question and which action the answer addresses. A practical case history can keep these distinctions clear even when the same events are described in more than one place. Use current official channels for status enquiries.

Ask what would confirm that your next step is complete

Write the action you need to take and what official response or status would show completion. Give yourself a date to check in through an appropriate route. If a qualified adviser is involved, confirm what the adviser has agreed to handle and what remains your responsibility. Retain confirmation in the private place you use for the relevant correspondence. Record received dates and any requests for further information. An accurate status note can make later decisions easier when you need to tell a reviewer whether there is an enquiry, an upcoming appointment or a documented filing.
