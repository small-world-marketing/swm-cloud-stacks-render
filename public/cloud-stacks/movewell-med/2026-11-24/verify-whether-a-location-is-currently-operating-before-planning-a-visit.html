Verify whether a location is currently operating before planning a visit

A checklist for confirming whether a Metro Vancouver mobility or healthy-aging location is actually open before you plan travel or book a visit.

Before arranging travel to any physical location for rehabilitation, longevity, or healthy-aging services, the first decision is simple but easy to skip: is that location actually open for appointments right now? Marketing pages, directories, and social posts can describe a site as upcoming, in development, or newly announced long before it accepts visitors. This checklist walks through how to confirm current operating status, who to ask, and what to record so a wasted trip or a misunderstanding about services does not happen. It does not evaluate any provider's quality or recommend a program; it only addresses the timing question.

Editorial date November 24, 2026. This guide offers general information for planning conversations. Ask a qualified clinician about individual care.

Why operating status is a separate question from service descriptions

A website, brochure, or social media post can describe a planned clinic, program, or expanded service area without that location being open to the public yet. Businesses commonly publish details about future sites alongside currently operating ones, and the two can be easy to confuse at a glance. A page that lists services such as longevity programs, mobility-and-balance support, arthritis-focused care, or IV therapy does not confirm that every listed location offers that service today, or that the location exists as a physical site at all. Before planning any visit, separate three questions: what services does an organization offer, where are those services currently delivered, and which specific address or location is presently operating.

Treating these as one question is the most common source of wasted trips. This checklist assumes nothing about any particular business's current status; it is a method for confirming status yourself, directly, close to the date of a planned visit, rather than relying on a page that may not reflect the most recent change.

Steps to confirm current status before you travel

Start by checking the organization's own current contact information rather than a cached or indexed version of their site, since cached pages can be weeks or months old. Call the listed phone number and ask directly: is this location currently open and accepting appointments at this address, as of today? Ask whether the service you want is delivered at that address or only at a different, currently operating site. If a representative describes a location as coming soon, opening soon, or in development, treat that as confirmation the location is not yet operating, regardless of how the website's homepage is phrased elsewhere.

Ask for the exact date range during which that status was last confirmed internally, since staff may also be working from outdated information. If you cannot reach a live person, request written confirmation by email before committing to travel time, parking, or time off work. Keep a note of who you spoke with and when, since this record becomes useful if a scheduling mismatch happens later and needs to be resolved with the organization directly.

What to do when information conflicts or feels unclear

When a phone call, website, and a booking confirmation give three different answers about whether a location is open, do not guess; ask for clarification in writing before you travel. A reasonable script is: can you confirm in an email that this address is currently operating and accepting in-person visits on the date of my appointment? Written confirmation protects you if a mismatch surfaces later, and it gives the organization a clear record to correct if their own listings are inconsistent. If a scheduling platform allows you to book an appointment at an address that turns out not to be operating, that is useful information to report back to the organization, since it suggests their systems are out of sync with reality.

For mobile or in-home services, the operating-status question is different: ask instead whether the service area includes your address and whether a clinician is currently accepting new in-home visits, since mobile availability can change independently of any fixed location's status. Avoid assuming that a physician-led or professionally described program automatically means every listed site is staffed and open.

Recording the answer so it holds up over time

Once you have a clear answer, write it down with the date, the person or channel that confirmed it, and the exact words used, such as open and accepting appointments or not yet open, coming soon. This record matters because operating status can change again before your appointment date, especially for newly announced locations. If your appointment is more than a few weeks away, plan to reconfirm status again closer to the date rather than relying on a single check made far in advance. If you are coordinating a visit for a family member or an older adult, share this written confirmation with them or with any support person attending the visit, so everyone arrives with the same expectation.

This record-keeping habit is not about distrust; it reflects the reality that service locations, staffing, and program availability can shift with little public notice. A short log of confirmed operating status, kept alongside appointment details, becomes a practical reference point if a dispute or a scheduling error needs to be sorted out later with the organization.

Questions this guide answers

How do I confirm a location is open before booking travel?

Call the organization directly and ask whether the specific address is currently open and accepting appointments, then request written confirmation by email. Website listings and cached search results can describe planned or upcoming locations without being current, so a direct, recent confirmation close to your visit date is the most reliable check.

Does a listed service category mean a location is currently operating?

No. A page describing services such as longevity, mobility-and-balance, arthritis, or IV programs does not confirm that a specific address is open today; service descriptions and location status are separate facts that need separate confirmation. Ask specifically about the address and date you plan to visit.

What should I do if a booking platform lets me schedule at a location that turns out to be closed?

Treat that as a reason to get written confirmation before traveling, and report the mismatch to the organization so they can correct their scheduling system. Do not rely on the ability to book an appointment as proof that a location is currently operating.
