Arthritis ProgramPort Coquitlam, BCChecklist

Verify eligibility, assessment, staffing, frequency, duration, fees, and exit terms

A checklist for confirming who qualifies, how a program is assessed, staffed, scheduled, priced, and ended before enrolling in any arthritis or rehabilitation program.

Before joining any mobile rehabilitation, arthritis, or healthy-aging program in Metro Vancouver, a reader needs clear answers about who the program accepts, how progress is assessed, which staff deliver care, and how the arrangement ends. These details rarely appear in marketing material and often differ between an initial consultation and the program actually delivered. This checklist organizes the seven questions worth confirming in writing before a first payment or signed agreement, with particular relevance to arthritis-focused programs where exercise tolerance, joint protection, and pacing vary widely between individuals.

Editorial date November 24, 2026. This guide offers general information for planning conversations. Ask a qualified clinician about individual care.

Eligibility and assessment criteria

A program's eligibility criteria determine whether a reader is even a reasonable candidate before any discussion of exercises or outcomes begins. For an arthritis-focused program, eligibility might depend on diagnosis confirmation, current medication use, recent imaging, or clearance from a physician, and a reader should ask exactly which of these the program requires versus merely recommends. Equally important is understanding how the initial assessment is structured: whether it includes a hands-on physical examination, a standardized questionnaire, a review of prior medical records, or simply a conversation. Readers should ask who performs the assessment, how long it takes, and whether the findings are documented in a report the reader can keep.

A program that cannot describe its assessment process in specific terms, or that skips assessment entirely before recommending a schedule of sessions, is harder to evaluate on its merits. Confirming these details separately from any registration step avoids assuming that acceptance into a program implies a particular diagnosis or guaranteed benefit.

Staffing, frequency, and duration

Readers should ask exactly which professional delivers each part of a program, since titles like physiotherapist, kinesiologist, or health coach refer to different scopes of practice and different regulatory oversight. A physical therapist's registration can be verified through the College of Health and Care Professionals of BC public registry at chcpbc.org, and a reader should ask whether the same registered therapist attends every session or whether staffing rotates. Frequency and duration questions matter just as much: how many sessions per week are proposed, over how many total weeks or months, and what happens if a reader needs to pause partway through.

Some programs are structured as fixed-length packages; others continue indefinitely until a reassessment. Readers should ask what triggers a reassessment and whether the frequency or duration can change without a new agreement or additional cost. Getting these specifics in writing, rather than relying on a verbal description during an initial call, gives readers a concrete basis for comparing one program's structure against another's before committing time or money.

Fees and what they include

A program's quoted fee should be broken down by component before a reader agrees to anything, because a single headline price can obscure separate charges for assessment, individual sessions, equipment, travel for mobile visits, or administrative fees. Readers should ask whether the fee covers a fixed number of sessions or is billed per visit, whether cancellation or rescheduling carries a charge, and whether any portion is eligible for reimbursement through extended health benefits, ICBC, or WorkSafeBC coverage. For arthritis programs specifically, readers should ask whether product costs such as braces, topical agents, or supplements are bundled into the program fee or billed separately, and whether any product recommendation comes with a specific brand or supplier relationship.

It is reasonable to request an itemized estimate in writing before the first paid session, and to ask what recourse exists if the actual cost exceeds the estimate. None of this requires evaluating whether the program's clinical approach is appropriate; it is strictly a financial transparency question that protects a reader regardless of which program they eventually choose.

Exit terms and what happens afterward

Exit terms describe what happens when a reader wants to stop a program, whether voluntarily or because it is not meeting expectations, and they deserve attention before enrollment rather than after a dispute arises. Readers should ask what notice period is required to end participation, whether any prepaid fees are refundable on a pro-rated basis, and whether records, assessment reports, or home exercise materials remain accessible after exit. For arthritis and mobility programs that involve an ongoing relationship with a mobile therapist, readers should also ask how a transition to another provider or back to a primary care physician is handled, including whether a summary report is provided.

If the program was presented as coordinating with a physician or pharmacist, readers should confirm whether that coordination continues after the reader exits or whether it was tied specifically to active participation. Asking these questions up front, and keeping the written answers alongside other program records, makes it far easier to leave a program cleanly if it no longer fits a reader's needs or circumstances.

Questions this guide answers

What should I confirm before accepting an arthritis program's price quote?

Ask for an itemized breakdown separating assessment fees, per-session charges, travel fees for mobile visits, and any product costs, since a single headline price can hide these components. Also confirm cancellation charges and whether extended health, ICBC, or WorkSafeBC coverage applies to any portion. Getting this in writing before the first paid session avoids disputes later.

How can I check who is actually delivering my sessions?

Ask the program directly which professional title will attend each session and whether staffing is consistent or rotates between visits. A physical therapist's registration status can be verified independently through the CHCPBC public registry at chcpbc.org. A named program lead does not confirm that every staff member delivering sessions holds the same credential.

What happens to my records if I stop a program early?

Ask in advance whether assessment reports, session notes, and any home materials remain accessible to you after exit, and whether a summary is sent to your physician or next provider. Programs vary in whether this continues automatically or only while actively enrolled. Confirming this before you start avoids losing documentation you may need for a second opinion.
