Organize Questions for a Nerve Block or Injection Consultation

Prepare for a clinician-led procedure conversation by clarifying its purpose, who will perform it, and what information travels to follow-up.

If a clinician has suggested a conversation about a nerve block or injection, ask what the practice wants to discuss and who will assess the question. The name alone leaves many practical details open, including the site of care, the professional involved, and how information from the visit will be shared. Write the question you want answered and identify any prior records the clinician should see. Prepare this outline for discussion with the qualified team, which can explain what the proposed care would mean in your situation.

Editorial date November 24, 2026. Use this guide to prepare questions for your appointment. Bring questions about your individual circumstances to a qualified clinician.

Confirm the purpose of the referral or conversation

Find out how the referring clinician described the reason for the visit and whether the receiving practice has that context. Ask whom you can contact if you are unsure what records have reached the team. Tell the receiving clinician which questions you want to discuss and ask what they plan to assess at the visit. An intake conversation, a consultation, and an appointment for a procedure may require different arrangements. Ask the practice what is scheduled so you can prepare accurately. Bring the original referral or order details if the office requests them.

Keep a short note of the date, the clinician involved, and the concern the referral was meant to address. That can help resolve confusion if the message passed through several offices.

Bring an accurate record of earlier care and open questions

If you have written records of earlier care, ask which documents the clinician wants to review and which details should appear in a short summary. Note when a report came from a different practice, especially if you are uncertain whether the receiving office has it. Include a current medicine list and any concerns you want the qualified team to discuss. When you describe a past event or experience, distinguish details you remember from what a written record confirms. Tell the team if an administrative or practical issue affected what happened.

Save copies of any records you share through a secure method. A short account with clearly sourced details lets a clinician ask for the right additional information when it matters to the discussion.

Ask the team about responsibilities and consent

If care is proposed, ask which clinician will explain it, who will provide it, and whom you will speak with if something changes. Ask what written information the practice offers and how you can review it before or during the consent discussion. Raise access concerns that could affect the plan and ask what support is available. If you have follow-up questions about a possible procedure, schedule time to ask them before making a decision. Keep the office contact and the relevant documents together so you can reach the right person easily.

If another practice wants to be informed, ask who will handle that communication and what record they will send. Take note of the responsible person and timing the team gives you.

Clarify who will review the outcome of the visit

Ask what happens after the consultation and who will explain the next step. Identify whether you will receive a visit note, a message, or other documentation and how you can request a copy. Ask the practice whom to contact if the referring clinician has not received the expected information. If the team wants you to prepare anything for another visit, write down the details and how to provide it securely. Record any timing instructions the treating clinician gives you and check whether those instructions also apply if the appointment moves.

Keep an open line in your notes for questions that belong with a clinician and another for administrative items such as referrals or billing. Review both before the next contact.
