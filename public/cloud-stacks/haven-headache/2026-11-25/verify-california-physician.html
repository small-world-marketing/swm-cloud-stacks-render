Check a California Physician’s Public License Record

Use the Medical Board of California’s verification resource to match the person, identify the record, and ask about anything that needs clarification.

If a practice names a physician who may see you, ask for the name and license information used in the public record. The Medical Board of California offers an official license verification resource that can help you find the corresponding physician record. The office can clarify which person is involved in a particular appointment. Keeping those questions separate makes it easier to confirm the record without treating a web biography or a scheduling message as the licensing source.

Editorial date November 24, 2026. Use this guide to prepare questions for your appointment. Bring questions about your individual circumstances to a qualified clinician.

Obtain the professional name before starting a search

Ask the practice for the physician’s full professional name and, when available, a license number or other details that can help identify the correct record. A common surname or a shared initial can return several people. Try the exact information the practice gives you and compare it with the result. If you see multiple possible matches, write down what you still need to confirm. You can return to the office with a specific question rather than choose the person whose name looks closest. Ask whether your appointment is with the person listed or with a different member of the practice.

An administrative confirmation can help you decide whose credential to verify before a visit.

Read the regulator’s entry as it appears today

Use the licensing authority’s site and record the date you checked it. Look for the identifying information and any status or other public information the page supplies. Record the wording you see and return to the regulator’s explanation if a term is unfamiliar. A partial search result may need a second step to open the full record. Ask the regulator what it can explain if the record looks incomplete or if you see information you do not know how to read. Use the office to confirm that the record pertains to the person you expect to see.

Keeping a date and a source lets you revisit the same entry if an appointment changes later.

Ask service and access questions through their own channels

Once you have identified a record, ask the practice who will do what during the visit, who can answer follow-up questions, and whether any service requires a separate contact. The license lookup serves a verification purpose and can be kept apart from appointment availability and insurance checks. If the practice uses a team, ask who is expected to participate and which regulator applies to each person’s professional role. Check network information with both the plan and office when that matters to your decision. Keep the answers in different fields on your worksheet.

This makes it easier to see which question remains outstanding instead of treating all of them as solved by a single search.

Record the result with just enough detail to repeat the check

Write the date, the source, the name you confirmed, and a brief note about any open item. Keep personal information in your own records and share it through a secure channel when needed. If the appointment is reassigned to a different person, request their information and repeat the check. If you encounter an unexplained difference, say what differs and ask the office or regulator to clarify it. A compact note can preserve the outcome without requiring you to save every page you visited. Add the date of any clarification and the person or office that supplied it.

Bring unresolved questions to the office before you rely on a scheduling description for an important decision.

Keep the check usable when you share it

If you are helping someone arrange their appointment, ask how much of the search result they want you to record and share. A brief note can identify the date, which official site you used, and the credential you matched. Leave any uncertain information clearly labeled as a question. Share the note with the person you are helping and let them decide how it should be used.
