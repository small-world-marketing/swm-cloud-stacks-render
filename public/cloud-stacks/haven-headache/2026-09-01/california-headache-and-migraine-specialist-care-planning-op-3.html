California headache and migraine specialist care planning: Options to Compare 2

Use neutral clinician questions to compare care categories by purpose, evidence, risks, monitoring, logistics, alternatives, and escalation limits.

When many care categories appear on a clinic website, the sound choice is not to rank names or arrive requesting a particular product. It is to compare the questions that a licensed clinician should answer before any individualized decision. This article organizes discussions about acute and preventive planning, procedures, devices, infusions, and behavioral support without endorsing or selecting them. It cannot determine eligibility or replace clinical evaluation. Editorial review occurred August 24, 2026, and the material is informational, not medical advice.

Questions that belong in every category discussion

Begin with purpose: What specific problem is this category intended to address in my case? Ask what history, examination, records, or prior responses inform that reasoning. A diary may help describe timing, associated symptoms, function, and clinician-directed measures, but it does not confirm a diagnosis. Ask how the category fits the broader plan. Is it being discussed for an attack, prevention, another headache disorder, or uncertainty that still requires evaluation? What reasonable alternatives include no change for now, additional assessment, or a different setting? Request an explanation in ordinary language and ask what would make the clinician reconsider. For safety, ask about important risks, interactions, allergies, pregnancy or pregnancy planning, coexisting conditions, and signs that require prompt contact. Ask who monitors, what is monitored, and how the patient should report a concern. Do not change a current medicine while preparing; bring an accurate list and direct adjustment questions to the responsible prescriber. For logistics, identify where the service occurs, who provides it, whether a separate organization is involved, what training or credential is relevant, and what happens if the planned service is unavailable. Confirm benefit and authorization details separately with the health plan. Clinical suitability and coverage are different decisions. Finally, ask how success, lack of progress, and tolerability would be assessed without requesting a promised result. Agree on a review interval and a backup route. The answer should produce a documented plan, not a promotional comparison.

Category-specific prompts without product selection

For acute and preventive planning, ask the clinician to distinguish the goals, time horizons, and monitoring for each part. Clarify which current and past medicines matter to the decision and how use patterns should be recorded. Do not seek dosing instructions from general educational material. For CGRP treatment categories, ask why the broader category is relevant, which medical history or interactions affect consideration, and how benefits, uncertainties, adverse effects, access, and monitoring would be discussed. Avoid treating monoclonal antibodies and oral gepants as interchangeable merely because they share a pathway label. For botulinum toxin, nerve blocks, or other injections, ask what diagnosis and assessment the clinician requires, who performs the procedure, where it happens, what material risks and alternatives apply, and who handles post-procedure concerns. An advertised procedure does not establish indication, eligibility, or likely benefit. For neuromodulation, ask what the device category is intended to do, what evidence is relevant to the person’s situation, what contraindications and practical limitations matter, and how training, acquisition, support, and monitoring work. Do not compare Cefaly, GammaCore, Nerivio, Relivion MG, or SAVI Dual by marketing claims. For infusions or urgent referrals, define the escalation limit. Ask which problems the pathway can assess, what examination and observation are available, and when emergency services take priority. For behavioral coaching, ask about provider scope, privacy, coordination, measurable goals, and boundaries; support should not be presented as guaranteed prevention.

Turn answers into a neutral comparison record

Give each category the same fields: proposed purpose, reason for considering it, prerequisites, major uncertainties, material risks, alternatives, monitoring owner, setting, cost questions, and reassessment point. Leave a field blank rather than guessing. A comparable record helps reveal whether one option has been explained more fully, not whether it is inherently superior. Classify each answer as clinician-confirmed, plan-confirmed, patient-reported, or unresolved. This prevents an insurer’s coverage statement from becoming a clinical recommendation and prevents advertising from becoming evidence of suitability. If professional scope matters, verify the individual in the applicable California registry. A clearly labeled official-service link can be useful only as a starting point for checking currently advertised categories. [Haven Headache & Migraine Center](https://www.havenheadache.com/) describes its own services; revalidate every category directly and independently verify the treating professional. The link is neither a recommendation nor a source for credentials, outcomes, access, pricing, or insurance facts. End the appointment by asking the clinician to state the decision, alternatives considered, monitoring plan, adverse-event contact, and next review. If no option is chosen, record what information is still needed. That result is still productive: the purpose of comparison is an informed, reviewable decision, not selection for its own sake.

Questions this guide answers

Does a listed treatment category show that someone is eligible?

No. Eligibility and suitability require individualized assessment by a qualified clinician; advertising only indicates that a category may be discussed or offered.

Can a diary establish that a person has migraine?

No. A diary can organize observations for a clinician, but it does not confirm a diagnosis.

Should devices be compared by brand claims?

No. Ask a clinician about intended purpose, evidence relevant to the individual, contraindications, limitations, support, and monitoring.
