replacement window and exterior project decisions Chicagoland: Cost and Scope Questions 3

Seven comparisons that help homeowners test whether exterior-project proposals describe equivalent work, allowances, and responsibilities.

Two totals are comparable only when they buy substantially equivalent work. An exterior proposal may bundle removal, opening repair, flashing, finishes, disposal, and adjustment; another may leave several of those items undefined. A lower figure can represent a narrower scope rather than better value, while a higher figure is not proof of better execution. The practical task is to normalize each offer before judging cost. The seven checks below form a selection framework for window, door, siding, or combined exterior work. They are not contractor rankings. Their purpose is to expose differences, unknowns, and financial decision points that should appear in writing before a homeowner authorizes work.

Seven scope comparisons to put beside every price

1. Match locations and quantities. Create one row for every opening, elevation, door, trim area, or accessory. Record identifying room or exterior labels, unit type, operation, approximate size, and whether adjoining work is included. A whole-house headline is insufficient when proposals count combinations, transoms, or joined units differently. 2. Separate product specification from installation scope. For windows and doors, request manufacturer, exact series or model, configuration, finish, glazing option, hardware, screens, grilles, jamb treatment, and label documentation where performance matters. Then list removal method, opening preparation, fastening, sealing, water-management integration, insulation, and adjustment independently. Product cost cannot reveal whether installation details match. 3. Identify retained and replaced materials. Determine whether interior casing, stools, extension jambs, exterior trim, siding, sheathing, flashing, thresholds, and wall finishes stay or go. Insert and full-frame window approaches expose different materials. Door projects can vary in treatment of the sill, frame, trim, and hardware. A clear boundary prevents assumptions about finished appearance. 4. Price uncertainty deliberately. Ask which concealed conditions could create added work, how labor and materials would be valued, who documents the finding, and whose written approval is required. Examples may include deteriorated substrate, damaged framing, irregular openings, or unexpected layers. Do not demand a fictional fixed outcome where inspection is impossible; demand a controlled change process. 5. Allocate household and site responsibilities. Compare furniture movement, shade removal, alarm coordination, landscape protection, dust barriers, daily cleanup, debris disposal, access, pets, parking, and restroom arrangements. Also note who protects an opening if weather interrupts work. Unassigned tasks often become surprise effort or disagreement. 6. Put payment and timing terms on the same grid. Record deposit, progress triggers, final-payment condition, estimated ordering window, dependencies, and cancellation language. Avoid comparing only projected start dates. Illinois consumer materials address written home-repair contracts and homeowner protections, while the Home Repair and Remodeling Act contains applicable statutory language. Read current official information and obtain qualified advice for legal questions rather than relying on a sales summary. 7. Compare completion evidence. Define final walkthrough, operation checks, finish review, cleanup, unresolved-item list, product information, warranty documents, receipts, change orders, and any permit or inspection records applicable to the project. A proposal should say who supplies each record and when.

How to choose without inventing certainty

After completing the grid, label every cell included, excluded, allowance, owner responsibility, or unanswered. Ask providers to clarify material differences in revised writing. Do not silently fill gaps based on conversation. If one proposal uses an allowance, compare both its amount and what happens when actual cost differs. If alternatives are offered, request separate pricing and scope changes so the base offer remains visible. Evaluate risk as well as total. A well-defined change-order procedure may be more useful than an unsupported promise that no hidden damage exists. Model-specific documentation is more informative than broad claims about panes, materials, or brands. A proposed efficiency improvement should reference the selected product’s ratings and relevant climate criteria, without guaranteeing energy savings. Likewise, warranty value depends on current written terms, covered components, exclusions, transfer rules, registration duties, and the party responsible for labor; the word lifetime alone is not a comparison. Before signing, reconcile the final contract against the normalized grid. Confirm business identification, project address, exact work, product details, price or pricing method, payment stages, approximate schedule framework, cleanup, warranties, cancellation information when applicable, and signatures. Preserve the proposal version incorporated into the agreement. If a salesperson says a detail will be handled later, decide whether that item is important enough to resolve before commitment. The best-supported choice is the one whose scope fits the diagnosed condition, whose unknowns have manageable procedures, and whose records let the homeowner verify what was promised. Cost remains important, but it becomes meaningful only after the work, responsibilities, and completion standard are visible.

Questions this guide answers

Why can two exterior-project proposals differ substantially in price?

They may specify different products, removal methods, retained finishes, repair allowances, site duties, disposal, documentation, or completion standards.

How should concealed damage be addressed before work starts?

The agreement should explain likely categories, documentation, pricing method, authorization, and how the schedule may change if a condition is discovered.
