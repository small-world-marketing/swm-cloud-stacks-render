Prepare Questions for a Health Care Consent Discussion

Organize what you want a provider to explain about a proposed service, alternatives, questions, support, and documenting decisions.

Before a service is proposed, write down what you would want explained so you can take part in the consent conversation. The B.C. Health Care (Consent) and Care Facility (Admission) Act is an official source for the province’s consent framework. The practitioner proposing care can explain how the process applies to the service under discussion. Keep this worksheet focused on your questions and the explanations you receive. If part of the conversation is hard to understand, ask the provider to explain it again in language you can use.

Editorial date November 24, 2026. Use this information to prepare for a conversation with a qualified clinician.

Ask the practitioner to describe the proposed service

Ask what they propose doing, why it is being discussed, and what part of the appointment will address it. If more than one person will be involved, ask who will explain the service and who would provide it. Ask how the practitioner will share information about relevant benefits, risks, and alternatives for your circumstances. Write the professional’s answer in your own words and check that you understood it correctly. If you need information in a different language or another format, ask what support is available. A clear explanation can be easier to review if you also know where to find a written version.

Ask how to contact the practitioner about a question that comes up after you leave the office.

Make it clear which choices you want to discuss

Identify anything you want more time to consider and what information would help. Ask what other options the practitioner wants you to know about, including what would happen if you chose to wait or sought more information. Keep the responses with the date and the person who gave them. Ask which practical decisions are separate, such as scheduling another visit, obtaining an estimate, or arranging transport. If a support person joins the discussion, decide which parts you want help with and what you want to discuss privately. Tell the practice how it should include them.

Ask whom you can reach if you change your mind about sharing information with the person. A record of your questions can give the team a clearer way to support your participation.

Clarify which permissions belong to records and communication

If the team wants to contact another office or share your records, ask what information is needed, who will receive it, and how you can tell what was sent. The process for sharing information and the conversation about a proposed service can create separate questions. Use a separate line for each. Ask how to obtain a copy of any form or record the practice wants to keep. If you place a limit on what should be shared, ask how the office records and communicates that instruction. Update the practice if a contact or preference changes.

Ask what secure channel it uses to work with another professional or support person. Keep private information with the office that needs it and give the person receiving it enough context to use it appropriately.

Check which decision the practitioner has recorded

Before you get started with a service, ask which step the practice believes you have agreed to and which questions remain open. If something in the plan changes, ask how it will be explained and documented. Take note of the directions you need to follow and whom to call for clarification. If the practice sends you a summary afterward, compare it with the points you recall. Ask the provider to clarify any difference that may affect your next appointment. Leave a dated field for something that was discussed and still needs confirmation.

This distinction lets a future clinician understand what was agreed and what remained a question. Keep any updated record in the place you use for current appointment information.

Ask where you can get an official explanation

For a question about the province’s framework, use the official B.C. law as a starting source and seek qualified advice if you need help applying it. For an appointment question, speak with the practitioner proposing care. Write down who can explain any step you have not understood and when to contact them.
