Questions when an injury inquiry moves between offices

Trace a legal inquiry from one office to another and verify the receiving contact before sending duplicates or additional private records.

A second office calls and says someone forwarded your injury inquiry. You recognize the name of the first contact but have never spoken with the caller. The first question is how the inquiry moved, not which person will eventually handle a case. A referral, a scheduling service, a joint engagement and a request for more information can involve different arrangements. Ask for a plain-language explanation before treating the second office as your lawyer.

Trace the handoff through independent contacts

Contact the first office through a route you independently verified. Ask whether it referred the inquiry, to whom, and for what purpose. Record the date, department and answer without repeating private medical or financial details in an ordinary message. If the first office says it sent a limited summary, ask which facts were included and who was meant to receive them. If it cannot confirm a transfer, preserve the incoming message and seek clarification before sending additional information.

Ask the second office for its public business name and the full name of the lawyer who would conduct a substantive discussion. Confirm the individual through the State Bar of California's public attorney profile. Similar names and business names can lead to confusion. A listing verifies facts displayed in that record. It does not establish an engagement, a particular fee arrangement, case acceptance or the identity of the person behind an unsolicited message.

Ask what the second office is offering

Distinguish a time to speak with intake staff from an appointment with a lawyer. Ask what the conversation covers, whether a charge is proposed, whether any documents are necessary at that stage and how the office deals with possible conflicts. A person can ask about the process before transmitting an entire file. If a lawyer needs more detail to address an individual question, ask how to provide the information through the office's verified channel.

A useful follow-up concerns overlapping instructions. What should you do with an earlier portal invitation or an upcoming appointment at the first office? Which office is the current contact for corrections? Has either firm offered a written representation agreement? Put the answers beside the date and source. If descriptions differ, ask the relevant office to explain the discrepancy. Do not infer a division of professional responsibility from the fact that two firms have communicated.

Keep original records associated with each transmission

In a fictional example, the first office has a four-item index and the second asks for one photograph. Give the photograph an identifier and record the specific version shared, the verified recipient and the confirmation. If a later request uses a different case number, identify the office that issued it. One person's folder can legitimately contain two reference numbers, and preserving the issuer prevents a later reader from treating them as competing versions of the same number.

For someone who wants to check contact details published by Ghassemi Law Group itself, the firm's official website is one source. It cannot authenticate a third party merely because that party mentions the firm. Bring unresolved questions about authority, confidentiality, fee sharing or the legal effect of a proposed engagement to a qualified California lawyer who can review the real facts. A good handoff ends with the next contact and purpose plainly identified, even when the decision about representation remains open.

When a second office already has some documents

Ask the receiving office to identify what it actually holds, using filenames or another limited inventory if appropriate. Match that list against the original transmission record. If a file appears that you do not recognize, ask where it came from and which matter it is associated with. Avoid sending an additional copy merely to see whether two offices agree. If the inquiry concerns confidentiality or the authority to transfer records, arrange for a qualified lawyer to address that question privately. A complete inventory can be useful even if you ultimately decide against an appointment.

Questions this guide answers

Does a referral establish representation?

Ask each office what role it has, whether it has offered an engagement and which writing, if any, would govern its work.

How can I tell which files were forwarded?

Request a limited inventory through verified contacts and compare names, versions and dates against your own transmission record.
