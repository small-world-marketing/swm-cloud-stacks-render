Orange County injury claim process and attorney selection: Options to Compare 2

Ask focused questions when comparing lawyers for collision, premises, animal, construction, severe-injury, or wrongful-death matters.

The useful comparison is between service models that fit the incident’s unresolved issues, not between broad labels such as “aggressive” or “full service.” Begin by identifying the setting, likely record sources, communications already received, and any special system that may be implicated. Then ask each lawyer the same questions about scope, staffing, information security, insurer contact, litigation capacity, and closeout. The questions below neither establish responsibility nor indicate that a civil claim should be filed. They are designed to expose differences that matter before a reader chooses whom to consult further. Any deadline, government-claim issue, coverage question, party identification, or procedural choice needs prompt advice based on the specific facts.

Questions that distinguish service options

First ask, “Which parts of this matter would your proposed engagement include, and which would it exclude?” Follow with incident-specific prompts. For a car, uninsured-driver, hit-and-run, motorcycle, bicycle, pedestrian, rideshare, truck, or bus event, ask who would examine policy documents, reporting records, vehicle ownership, employment information, app status, or public-entity questions where relevant. For premises, dog-bite, boating, or construction incidents, ask how the office identifies the people or organizations connected to control, ownership, work status, or record custody without reaching premature legal conclusions. Next ask who performs each task. Who is the responsible lawyer? Which communications go to a case manager or assistant? Who reviews an insurer request, authorization, release, recorded-statement request, or proposed settlement document? Who would supervise pleadings, discovery, depositions, expert work, mediation, trial, or an appeal if those stages became appropriate? A lawyer need not promise that any stage will occur; the comparison concerns the office’s disclosed capacity and engagement boundaries. Ask how records enter the file. Can the office accept photographs, medical invoices, employment records, property-loss material, police information, DMV records, witness details, and a communication log through a secure method? How are duplicates, corrected documents, and originals handled? What acknowledgement should the client expect after sending something? The answer should describe a process, not encourage readers to obtain material unlawfully. Never trespass, interfere with property, contact someone in a prohibited manner, or risk safety to create evidence. Communication questions should address substance. What events trigger an update? Who communicates an offer or material request? How can a client correct a factual error? How are language, disability, or technology needs discussed? Do not infer accessibility or availability; ask the office directly and document its answer. Finally, ask about conflicts, fee terms, costs, liens, withdrawal, discharge, file return, and closing records. Request time to read the proposed agreement and ask where oral explanations appear in writing. No generic checklist can interpret that contract.

Compare answers without turning them into endorsements

Organize the responses under four headings: verified, stated, written, and unresolved. “Verified” can include the lawyer’s California license information found through the State Bar search. “Stated” captures a consultation answer not yet reflected in an agreement. “Written” points to a proposal, policy, or follow-up message. “Unresolved” protects against guessing. State Bar consumer resources, reviewed September 1, 2026, can support verification and interviewing; they do not rank candidates for a particular reader. Compare options at the level of actual need. A severe burn, amputation, spinal injury, or death may generate extensive health, privacy, authority, and long-term record questions, but it would be improper to predict care, damages, claim value, or result. Ask how the lawyer would organize complex records and coordinate any separately qualified professionals. For a wrongful-death consultation, ask who may communicate with the office and what authority documents it needs; do not assume family roles or entitlement. Reject shortcuts based on advertised recoveries, awards, rankings, supposed insider status, or guaranteed speed. Also avoid assuming that a lawyer’s stated practice area proves acceptance. A good comparison leaves room for “not determined.” It records whether the lawyer understood the issue, disclosed the proposed scope, and explained who would do the work. Questions this guide answers

Does a listed practice area mean the lawyer will accept my matter?

No. It describes stated scope only. Acceptance, conflicts, staffing, and engagement terms must be confirmed directly.

How should consultation answers be compared?

Separate independently verified facts, oral statements, written terms, and unresolved points, then apply the same questions to every candidate.
