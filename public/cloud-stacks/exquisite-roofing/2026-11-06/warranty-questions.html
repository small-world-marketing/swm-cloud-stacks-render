Roof Warranty Questions to Ask Before Signing

Learn the difference between product and workmanship warranty terms so you can ask informed questions before signing a roofing contract.

Roof warranty questions usually come up after a homeowner has already compared estimates and is close to choosing a contractor. The core decision at this stage is not whether a warranty exists, but understanding what kind of warranty is being offered, what it actually covers, and who is responsible if something goes wrong later. Roofing warranties generally fall into two separate categories: a manufacturer's product warranty covering defects in materials like shingles, and a separate workmanship warranty from the installing contractor covering how the roof was installed. These two things are not interchangeable, and a homeowner who treats them as one and the same can end up confused later if an issue arises.

This article does not state or imply any specific warranty length, coverage terms, or guaranteed outcome for a project at a particular property. Instead, it focuses on the questions worth asking so that whatever is offered can be evaluated clearly before a contract is signed. Some manufacturer-backed coverage can also include installation-related protection under specific conditions. Read the actual documents and identify who is responsible for each type of issue before comparing offers.

What is the difference between a product warranty and a workmanship warranty?

A product warranty is issued by the shingle or roofing material manufacturer and generally addresses defects in the materials themselves, not installation quality. GAF's homeowner education resources describe the components that make up a complete roof system, including underlayment, flashing, ventilation, and the shingles themselves, and note that how these parts work together affects overall performance. A workmanship warranty, by contrast, is offered by the contractor who performs the installation and addresses whether the work was done correctly according to manufacturer instructions and local building practices. These two warranties can have different durations, different claim processes, and different companies responsible for honoring them.

Asking a contractor to clearly separate these two categories in writing, rather than referring to 'the warranty' as a single undefined term, is one of the most useful clarifying questions a homeowner can ask.

Ask which exclusions, installation requirements, registration steps and maintenance conditions apply to each offer. Have the contractor identify these provisions in the actual documents. Avoid assuming that one missed requirement removes all coverage, or that a product brand alone establishes what is covered; the written terms determine the particular coverage being offered.

A homeowner who wants to understand what is being installed and why certain components matter can review GAF's roof repair guide for a general explanation of how repairs interact with existing roofing systems. This does not replace a contractor's project-specific explanation, but it provides useful background vocabulary before that conversation happens.

What should be confirmed in writing before signing?

Before signing a contract, ask the contractor to put the specific scope of warranty coverage in writing rather than relying on a verbal summary. This means getting clarity on what components are covered, what situations are excluded, what maintenance or inspection steps (if any) are the homeowner's responsibility, and how a claim would be initiated if an issue is noticed later. Because this article does not state any particular warranty duration or coverage guarantee, homeowners should treat every number and condition as something to verify directly with the contractor and, where relevant, the manufacturer's own documentation for the specific product being installed.

It is also reasonable to ask whether the workmanship warranty is transferable if the home is sold, and whether it depends on using only that contractor for any future repairs. If an enhanced manufacturer warranty is offered, ask for the exact name of that coverage, its eligibility requirements and evidence that registration will be completed. Ask separately how later repairs or a property sale could affect it; those answers should come from the written manufacturer and installer terms.

This resource does not address warranty terms directly, but it provides a path for understanding contractor accountability more broadly, which is a useful complement to warranty-specific questions.

Questions this guide answers

Is a manufacturer product warranty the same as a contractor's workmanship warranty?

No, they are generally separate. A manufacturer's product warranty typically covers defects in the roofing materials themselves, while a workmanship warranty comes from the installing contractor and addresses installation quality; ask for both to be described in writing separately rather than assuming one covers the other.

Which conditions could affect roofing warranty coverage?

Ask for the actual warranty documents and identify their installation requirements, exclusions, maintenance conditions and any registration steps. These vary by product and coverage option. A contractor should point to the applicable provisions, and unresolved questions should be confirmed with the company responsible for the warranty.

Where can I check a contractor's licensing status in Maryland?

The Maryland Home Improvement Commission maintains consumer resources, including licensing and complaint information, that homeowners can use to review a contractor's standing before signing a contract. This resource addresses licensing rather than warranty terms, so it should be used alongside, not instead of, direct warranty questions to the contractor.
