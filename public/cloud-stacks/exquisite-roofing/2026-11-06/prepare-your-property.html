Preparing Your Property for Roofing Work

A practical checklist for coordinating access, parking, pets, landscaping and indoor protection before a roofing crew begins work at your home.

Preparing your property before a roofing crew arrives is mostly about coordination, not labor. The goal is to give the crew clear, safe access to the roof and the areas around your home while protecting vehicles, landscaping, pets and the inside of the house from dust and vibration. Most of this preparation can be handled in the days leading up to the project through a short conversation with your contractor about their specific logistics needs.

Because every property layout, driveway configuration and landscaping situation is different, the checklist below covers common preparation categories rather than a fixed universal routine. Use it as a starting point for a conversation with your roofing contractor about what their crew will need on the scheduled day, including how they plan to protect siding, gutters and plantings during tear-off and material delivery.

Clearing Access, Parking and Delivery Areas

Roofing projects typically involve a delivery truck for materials, a dumpster or trailer for tear-off debris, and parking space for the crew's vehicles. Ask your contractor in advance how much driveway or street space they expect to need, and move personal vehicles to an alternate location for the day if the driveway will be used for staging or a dumpster drop.

Driveways and walkways near the work area should stay clear of toys, planters, hoses and other items that could be damaged by falling debris or foot traffic. If the crew needs to set up ladders or equipment along one side of the house, confirm which side in advance so you can clear that specific area rather than the entire yard.

If your property has a shared driveway, a narrow street, or limited turnaround space for a delivery truck, mention this when scheduling the project so the contractor can plan logistics accordingly. Homeowners associations or local parking rules may also affect where a dumpster or trailer can sit for multiple days, which is worth checking before the project date.

Protecting Landscaping, Gutters and Outdoor Fixtures

Falling debris during tear-off can affect plants, garden beds, outdoor furniture and fixtures located close to the house. Moving portable items such as potted plants, grills, patio furniture and decorative yard pieces away from the foundation gives the crew room to work and reduces the chance of accidental damage.

For landscaping that cannot be moved, such as established shrubs or garden beds near exterior walls, ask your contractor what protective measures they typically use, such as tarps or plywood coverage, and whether a qualified tree-care provider should address overhanging branches before the project begins. This is a reasonable question to raise during scheduling rather than something to guess at.

Gutters, downspouts, window screens and exterior light fixtures near the roofline can also be affected by tear-off debris. Pointing out any fixtures you are particularly concerned about, such as a recently installed light or a delicate trellis, gives the crew useful information before work starts rather than after an issue occurs.

Managing Pets, Noise and Indoor Protection

Roofing work involves sustained noise, vibration and foot traffic on the roof, which can be stressful for pets and difficult to work around for household routines. Keeping pets in a separate part of the home, a boarding situation, or with a family member for the day is generally more comfortable for the animal and safer for the crew moving equipment around the property.

Inside the home, vibration from tear-off and nailing can occasionally dislodge items from walls or shelves, particularly in attic spaces or top-floor rooms directly under the roof. Taking down fragile wall hangings, mirrors or shelf items in upper-floor rooms before the project begins is a simple precaution. If stored items may be exposed to dust, discuss protection with the contractor. Limit your preparation to readily accessible finished areas; leave entry into unfinished attic spaces and any work near wiring or insulation to the appropriate professional.

Finally, let your contractor know about household considerations that could affect scheduling within the day, such as a work-from-home video call, a sleeping infant, or a home office that needs to stay relatively quiet during certain hours. While roofing work is inherently noisy, sharing this information in advance allows the crew to plan their day with your household's needs in mind where it is practical to do so.

Questions this guide answers

Do I need to move my car before roofing work starts?

In most cases, yes, if your driveway will be used for material staging, a dumpster, or crew parking. Ask your contractor how much space they expect to need so you can plan alternate parking for the day, since every property's layout is different.

Should I keep pets inside during a roofing project?

Keeping pets in a separate area of the home, or arranging off-site care for the day, is generally more comfortable for them given the noise and vibration involved, and it also reduces the chance of a pet wandering near equipment or debris staging areas on the property.

What should I do with landscaping near the house before tear-off?

Move portable items like potted plants and furniture away from the foundation, and ask your contractor about protective coverage for landscaping that cannot be relocated, such as established shrubs close to the exterior walls.
