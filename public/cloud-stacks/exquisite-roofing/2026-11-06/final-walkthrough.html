A Homeowner Checklist for the Roofing Walk-Through

A practical checklist for reviewing scope, cleanup and handover paperwork during the final roofing walk-through before closing out a project.

The final walk-through is the point where a homeowner and contractor confirm that the completed roofing work matches the agreed scope before the project is considered closed out. This is a documentation and verification step, not a technical roof inspection performed by the homeowner. The goal is to compare what was promised in writing against what was actually delivered, note any cleanup or finishing items that still need attention, and collect the paperwork that will matter later if a question or warranty issue comes up.

Because the walk-through happens at ground level and from accessible vantage points, it will not substitute for a contractor's own quality checks on the roof surface itself. Homeowners should treat this meeting as a chance to ask questions, review documents, and flag visible concerns from the yard or finished rooms, rather than as an opportunity to personally verify installation details that require being on the roof.

Compare the finished work to the written scope

Start by pulling out the original proposal or contract and reading through the listed scope of work line by line. Confirm that the materials installed match what was specified, including shingle brand and style if that was part of the agreement, and that any extras such as new flashing, ventilation components, or decking repairs discussed during the project were actually completed.

If the scope changed at any point, for example because decking damage was discovered once the old roofing was removed, there should be a written change order or similar documentation describing what changed and why. A homeowner reviewing the final walk-through should ask to see that documentation rather than relying on a verbal explanation, since written records are what will matter if a disagreement comes up later.

It also helps to walk the perimeter of the house and look at visible roof edges, gutters, and downspouts from the ground to confirm that these areas appear to have been addressed if they were part of the scope. This is a visual check from a safe vantage point, not a substitute for the contractor's own completion review.

Check cleanup and site restoration

Roofing projects generate debris, and a thorough cleanup is a normal part of project closeout. Walk the yard, driveway, and any garden beds near the house to look for leftover nails, shingle fragments, or packaging material. Many contractors use magnetic sweepers for nail debris, and it is reasonable to ask whether that step was completed.

Look at landscaping, gutters, and any outdoor fixtures that were near the work area to confirm they were not damaged during the project or that any agreed protective measures were removed and the area restored. If a dumpster or material staging area was used, confirm it has been removed and the surface underneath is in acceptable condition.

Indoors, if attic access was used during the project, it is reasonable to ask whether insulation or attic contents were disturbed and, if so, how they were handled. These are observational checks a homeowner can reasonably make without climbing on the roof or handling any disturbed materials directly.

Collect and organize handover paperwork

The final walk-through is also the right time to collect paperwork that documents the completed project. This typically includes a copy of the final invoice matching the agreed scope, any manufacturer product information for the materials installed, and documentation describing workmanship or product coverage terms if those were part of the agreement.

Homeowners planning to keep a long-term maintenance record should ask for copies of any permit documentation and final inspection sign-off if the project required a permit through Anne Arundel County. Use Anne Arundel County's residential permit resources to locate the appropriate office and confirm which inspections or completion records apply to your specific permit.

Keeping these documents together in one folder, physical or digital, makes it easier to reference them later if a question comes up about what was installed, when, and under what terms. This record-keeping habit is especially useful if the home is sold in the future and a buyer or inspector asks about the roofing work.

Questions this guide answers

What should I compare during a final roofing walk-through?

Compare the completed work against the written scope in the original contract, including materials, any agreed extras, and documentation for any change orders made during the project. This written comparison matters more than a visual inspection from the ground, since many installation details are not visible or safely checkable without being on the roof.

Do I need to inspect the roof myself during the walk-through?

No, homeowners should not climb onto the roof to inspect completed work. The walk-through is meant for reviewing paperwork, checking cleanup and site restoration from ground level, and asking questions, while technical verification of the roofing itself is the contractor's responsibility.

What paperwork should I receive at project closeout?

You should expect a final invoice matching the agreed scope, manufacturer product information for installed materials, any workmanship or product coverage documentation, and permit or inspection records if the project required a permit. Keeping these together creates a useful reference if questions arise later.

What if cleanup was incomplete during the walk-through?

If debris, nails, or site restoration items are still outstanding, note them specifically and ask for a timeline to address them before considering the project fully closed. Addressing cleanup items before final sign-off is easier than trying to resolve them after the contractor has left the project as complete.
